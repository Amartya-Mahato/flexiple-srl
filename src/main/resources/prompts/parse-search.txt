A recruiter has described who they are looking for, in their own words:

"""
{{query}}
"""

Turn that into two things: objective filters the system can apply mechanically, and a subjective fit
rubric that captures what an excellent candidate looks like for this role.

CONTEXT ABOUT THE TALENT POOL

Locations that exist in the data:
{{known_locations}}

Skills that exist in the data (use these spellings wherever the recruiter means one of them):
{{known_skills}}

Company backgrounds are exactly these four values: startup, scaleup, enterprise, agency.

HOW TO WRITE THE OBJECTIVE FILTERS

- skills: only concrete, checkable technologies or tools the recruiter actually asked for. If they
  said "RDS", use the pool's spelling "AWS RDS". Do not pad the list with adjacent technologies the
  recruiter did not mention; two or three precise skills beat eight speculative ones.
- skills_match_all: true only if the recruiter clearly requires every skill. Default to false.
- min_years_experience / max_years_experience: always return both fields. If the recruiter gave a
  range or a bound, encode it exactly: "4-7 years" is min 4 and max 7; "at least 5" is min 5 and max
  null; "up to 3" is min null and max 3. Only use null for a bound the recruiter did not give.
  Never silently drop a stated range. "Senior" on its own is not a number - leave both null and put
  the seniority expectation in the rubric instead.
- locations: the city or region for the role. Omit entirely if the recruiter did not name one.
- remote_ok: true only if the recruiter said remote is acceptable.
- company_types: the background the recruiter wants the candidate to be in right now.
- past_company_types: use this when the recruiter says "has worked at", "has experience at" or
  "background in" rather than "currently at". Saying someone "has worked at startups" is usually a
  past_company_types requirement, not a current one - but if in doubt, prefer past_company_types
  because it is the more forgiving of the two.
- exclude_company_types: only when the recruiter explicitly rules a background out.

Be deliberately generous with objective filters. They are a hard gate: anything you add here removes
people permanently. Preferences, seniority feel, domain flavour and anything with a "prefer",
"ideally" or "strong" in it belongs in the rubric, not in the filters.

HOW TO WRITE THE FIT RUBRIC

- summary: one or two sentences describing the ideal person for this specific role.
- criteria: between three and five, each with a short name, a concrete description of what excellent
  looks like, and a weight between 0 and 1. Weights should sum to roughly 1.
- Cover what the recruiter cares about that a filter cannot check: depth versus breadth, ownership,
  domain, environment, trajectory, seniority.
- Do not restate a filter as a criterion unless there is real judgement in it. "Lives in Bangalore"
  is a filter. "Has run production databases they were on call for" is a rubric criterion.
